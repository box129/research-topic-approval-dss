The colour rule, stated once and exactly

Colour must never imply an academic verdict where none exists. That is the whole rule. It is not “green means Approve exclusively” — institutional green remains available for ordinary primary product interactions. It is a prohibition on green reaching a similarity classification, an absence state, or a non-academic lifecycle bucket; on amber reaching Pending; and on red reaching HIGH or an unavailable result.

LOW similarity never inherits approval semantics. Lightest chip weight, originality denial promoted into the lead block at 15px, record title at 500 weight because it is not a finding of substance, and the section states plainly that none is closely related.

PARTS 3–6

Board implementation contracts

Part 3 · Board A — similarity evidence
Terminology
Remove “risk” from every similarity surface. ADVISORY RISK LEVEL → “Similarity classification”. “High / Medium / Low Risk” → “Higher / Moderate / Lower similarity”. “5 matches returned” → “Closest stored records”, ranked 01–0n. At LOW the section states “none is closely related”. API tokens HIGH/MEDIUM/LOW and their thresholds are unchanged and remain visible as a subordinate mono chip.
Classification treatment
Neutral ink ramp, three fill weights, dashed for not-classified; label ≥14px (up from 12px). Distinguishable in greyscale and by label alone.
Raw cosine
Provenance strip and per-record meta line, mono 13px, always labelled “cosine”, always with the non-probability disclaimer at 13.5px and ≥4.5:1. Never a display figure. Exact numeric format is blocked on the F-1 contract — show the raw stored value; do not normalise, round or hide the inconsistency to make a screen look finished.
Icons
No icon on any similarity result; rank ordinals replace the ⚠. Warning iconography reserved for genuine system failure. No emoji anywhere.
Affordances · focus
Similarity evidence must not use approval/action green as a resting semantic treatment where that would give the evidence academic meaning — disclosure links are underlined ink, the retry is filled ink. Interactive focus and keyboard states follow the global accessible focus system; visible focus is never weakened to keep the component neutral.
Disclosure as emphasis
Higher opens all records; moderate and lower open the top record with the count visible. This mechanism replaces colour-as-urgency: the interface allocates attention instead of issuing a rating.
Provenance
Only fields the API supplies: title, population, location, study focus, supervisor, session, source tier, cosine. No field-level semantic attribution — the model does not score fields individually and the UI must not imply it does.
Mobile
Two container levels; chip below the title, not beside it; provenance as a tinted key/value strip; context fields as labelled lines. The existing Check My Topic mobile composition is protected — pixels proved it good.
Lecturer embedding
Record width, ~380px tall, banner and summary merged into one block, one record open by default, “Run a new check” inside the evidence block with its own scope note, plus “recorded ⟨timestamp⟩ · by ⟨lecturer⟩” which the student pre-check does not show. Interacts with Part 5: the provider-unavailable state replaces the evidence block entirely rather than appearing beneath it.
Part 4 · Board B — lecturer review detail
Geometry
A single vertical axis at record width. No side column, so no gutter can outlive its content — this retires the ~1,100px dead left gutter. Two container levels (page → section); nothing inside a section gets its own border. Context becomes a ruled grid, not seven bordered boxes.
Pending order
proposal identity + structured context → similarity evidence → evidence history (one summary line) → rationale → decision controls. Decision block is the only coloured region and the only one with a 2px rule above it; rationale field at a 720px measure.
Decided order
Submission identity (compact header) → decision record → proposal context → supporting similarity evidence → history. Outcome at 27px with decider and timestamp beside it, rationale at 17px directly beneath. The identity header keeps the decision attached to the topic decided; it is a header, not a section — no context grid.
Terminal controls
Disabled decision buttons removed; the decision record is the status cue. The explanation copy is preserved verbatim as a quiet line inside the decision block. A control that cannot be used is not information.
Rationale contract
Approve optional · Request Revision required · Reject required. Stated as a visible three-row table above the buttons and echoed in the confirmation dialog. Helper copy: “Required when rejecting a topic or requesting a revision, so the student knows what to change.”
History register
One row per check at ~40px — checked-at, classification, cosine, tier counts, author — collapsed to a summary line while pending, expanded on a terminal record. Purely typographic compression: nothing merged, nothing omitted, no backend change. Three columns at 390px with the rest per-row on demand. Do not group repeated checks without separate engineering approval — grouping would hide F-2 at the moment someone might notice it.
No unproven linkage
The evidence block reads “Latest saved similarity check” with its own timestamp — never “before the decision”, which asserts a snapshot↔decision relationship no data contract establishes. Ordering by timestamp is not linkage. Summary lines carry counts and dates only, never derived classification aggregates.
390px actions
All three decision actions full-width at 44px and stacked, never wrapped, constrained inside the visible column. A wrapped Reject was clipped in an earlier draft — that is the defect this rule prevents.

Part 6 · Board D — primitives
Badge families
Chip (classification) and pill (workflow) are separate components with separate colour vocabularies. Operational state is neither — an edge rule or inline text. Both pills and row actions carry white-space: nowrap; a control or pill that breaks across two lines stops reading as one.
Confirmation anatomy
One shell: action-specific title, concise consequence, rationale echo, quiet outlined Cancel, confirm button inheriting the action's semantics. Modals are the only floating surface — the whole elevation budget. Grammar fixed to “Request revision for this submission?”.
Shared record grammar
Primary identity → status/classification → labelled context → metadata → action, in that order. One 600-weight run per record; labels colour-differentiated, never bold; 10px outer radius; #e5e7eb borders; no inner borders; metadata floor 13.5px.
Two primitives, not one
Workflow record: bordered row, workflow pill, one primary action right-most, user-controlled ordering, rare technical metadata. Evidence record: ruled list row with no border of its own, ordinal-led, classification chip, no action, machine-controlled order that is never re-sortable, cosine expected. They share a grammar, not a template — and must not become one React component.
Responsive rule
Structured desktop record → stacked mobile record. Topic Repository is a bordered card at both viewports — desktop uses an internal multi-column grid; 390px collapses that interior vertically. It is not a table-to-card transformation. Routes that are tables may adopt a related strategy where pixels or source prove they are tables.
Column sizing
The action column is sized to its widest label; metadata columns are sized to their longest real value; only the fluid identity column absorbs the difference. Per Board B, the remedy for a broken email is room — overflow-wrap: break-word as a last resort, never anywhere, which is what produces “edu.n / g”.
Type roles
Page/record title 20–24/600 · section title 16/600 · record title 15.5/600 · field label 13–14/400 · value 14.5–15/400 · metadata 13.5 · helper 13.5–14 · eyebrow 11–11.5/500 · technical token 12.5–13 mono. Task-essential and interpretive text ≥13px; 11–12.5px reserved for nonessential structural labels and technical metadata. Everything ≥4.5:1. Only two roles uppercase, both nonessential. No 700+ weight in the workspace. See Part 12 C-3 — this conflicts with DESIGN.md §6.
Colour roles
Nine roles with explicit allowed/prohibited contexts per Part 2. Every colour use names a role; mapping roles onto tokens is implementation. Gold's four current jobs collapse to one — focus. See Part 12 C-2 — this conflicts with DESIGN.md §7.

PART 9

Functional / data defects

Carried separately. None may be disguised as a design change. Exactly as established in the audit — no speculative additions.

F-1 · Similarity values persisted on two scales
A fresh lecturer check on submission #6 stores 0.623 and its history renders “0.6%”, while older seeded snapshots render “81.4%”. One formatter is receiving both 0–1 floats and 0–100 values, so a 0.623 similarity is understated by two orders of magnitude — and both appear on the same screen ~700px apart.
ENGINEERING / DATA-CONTRACT INVESTIGATION REQUIRED BEFORE FINAL NUMERIC PRESENTATION
Establish one stored/display contract, then re-capture. Separately, and regardless of the scale chosen: a large percentage invites reading as duplication, originality or probability, so classification stays primary and the number subordinate.
F-2 · Duplicate persisted snapshots
Submission #5 carries six saved checks, four identical to the minute (22 May 20:16), same classification, cosine and tier counts. The compact register makes this more visible, deliberately. Do not group runs to tidy it away until the cause is known.
F-3 · Contradictory counts on one screen
“4 matches” and “Total Matches Found 5” render simultaneously. Which is correct is an engineering definition, not a display choice; the spec shows a single “records returned” figure and does not adjudicate.
F-4 · Directive advice stored in snapshots
Generated text such as “Request topic modification or check with colleagues” and “Coordinate with Dr. Adebayo before proceeding to avoid duplicate approvals” is persisted against submissions. Removing it from the surface does not remove it from the database — the generator must change too.

Implementation sequence

Every PR re-captures at 1440 and 390 at minimum; DESIGN.md §21 additionally requires 1280 and 768, which no board covered — see Part 12 C-5.

Categories: similarity persistence, response mapping, the snapshot copy generator, shared formatter. No frontend visual files.

Acceptance: a written contract stating stored scale, display scale and conversion point; a re-captured pack showing one consistent scale; snapshots free of directive advice. The lesson this process earned, carried explicitly

A correction is not complete until the rendered specimen, the normative rule, the summary documentation and the implementation all agree.

This was caught three times in review — Board B's note still arguing for phrasing that had been banned; Board C's CURRENT vs PROPOSED panel still advertising two withdrawn claims as features; Board D's artwork still ink after the prose went green. In each case the design was right and a description was wrong, or the reverse. Both fail identically for whoever implements from it. Treat it as a required review step per PR, not a habit to hope for.
