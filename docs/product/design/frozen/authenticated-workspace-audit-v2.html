Authenticated product audit · v2 · pixel-validated

Revised against 28 real captures

I inspected 15 of the 28 captures closely, chosen to test the specific findings you listed — including 5 of the 6 mobile captures. The 13 I have not opened are named in Part 3. Nothing implemented — no React, backend or DESIGN.md changes, no screens redrawn.

Headline — the pixels changed my mind in both directions

Two of my loudest findings were wrong. The "no eligible corpus" state I called the product's biggest gap is in fact its best-handled state. The mobile table overflow I flagged across seven routes does not happen — admin tables recompose into genuine record cards. Both retracted below.

And the central finding is materially worse than source could show. It is not only that HIGH is red. The screen says "ADVISORY RISK LEVEL" and "High Risk"; raw cosine 0.721 is the largest number on the page; every match carries a ⚠ warning triangle — including "Low Match"; and stored snapshots contain directive advice like "Request topic modification or check with colleagues." The system does not merely colour a verdict. It words one.

The biggest composition failure is one I never predicted: on a decided submission, six near-duplicate HIGH history rows consume roughly 1,000px and push the stored lecturer rationale — the most important text on the page — to the very bottom, below a set of disabled buttons.

Pixel-validated strengths

The empty-corpus state

Score reads N/A not 0. Classification reads "No Risk Classification" with a NOT CLASSIFIED chip. The panel is neutral, not green. Mobile table recomposition

Topic Repository at 390px is not a scrolling table. Nine records render as stacked cards, each with lifecycle pill, title, keywords, category, session, supervisor and data-quality line. No horizontal scroll, no clipping.

This is precisely the pattern I proposed a board to invent. It exists and it is good. Protect it and propagate it — it becomes the reference, not the deliverable.

Honest data

Missing values named, not hidden

"Matric number — Not provided". "Academic session — Not provided". "No matric number on record". Revision view: "No feedback was recorded with this request." Decided view: "No additional check result."

Nothing is faked, blanked or em-dashed away. This is a mature habit and it is consistent across roles.

Identity contract

"Personal email", matric-first

Review Detail labels the field PERSONAL EMAIL — not university email. Matric number appears before it in the grid. Bulk onboarding is described as matric-first.

My PT-2 concern is disproved in every capture I checked. The workspace already states the contract correctly; only the landing page had it wrong.

Restraint

Lecturer dashboard has no metric wall

One chip and one queue-preview card. No row of tinted MetricCards, no fabricated chart, no invented adoption figures. My C-2 traffic-light-dashboard fear does not materialise here.

It is arguably too thin — see Part 3 — but the restraint is the right starting point and much easier to build up than to strip back.

Copy

Boundary copy — but only that kind

Protect the boundary copy: "Advisory pre-check … does not save or submit your topic." · "Similarity evidence is advisory. Final decisions remain lecturer-controlled." · "This is not a final approval decision." · "Required when rejecting a topic or requesting a revision."

Do not protect directive machine advice. Stored snapshots also say "Request topic modification or check with colleagues" and "Coordinate with Dr. Adebayo before proceeding to avoid duplicate approvals". That is generated output instructing a lecturer on an academic decision — the opposite of boundary copy, and it must be rewritten or removed (F-4).

The distinction matters: one kind of sentence defends human authority, the other usurps it. My v1 "protect all advisory copy" conflated them.

PART 5

Responsive verdict

Confirmed mobile problems

Nested padding destroys the measure. On Review Detail at 390px, four levels of card inset leave roughly four words per line; one match title runs six lines. This is the dominant mobile defect and it is inherited from desktop nesting, not from the viewport.

Summary mini-cards stack into a tower. Risk Level / Highest Similarity / Total Matches become three tall boxes carrying three short values — the least efficient possible use of a 390px column, and duplicated content besides.

Decision buttons wrap awkwardly — Approve and Request Revision on one line, Reject orphaned below, all visually small. Target size unresolved without a DOM measurement.

The cosine figure dominates more at 390px than at 1440. On the HIGH mobile capture 0.721 in red is the single largest element on the screen, above the classification it is supposed to support.

Disproved source concerns

No horizontal overflow in any of the 5 mobile captures I opened (Review Detail, Topic Repository, and the HIGH / empty-corpus / provider-unavailable check states). Only submit-populated-mobile is unverified. My seven-route table-scroll finding is withdrawn.

No clipping. The Review Detail proposal, evidence and decision all render fully at 390px.

Check My Topic at 390px is genuinely good — and this corrects my own diagnosis. It holds only two container levels, keeps 8–10 words per line, and stacks five match records cleanly. The same evidence component composes well here and badly on Review Detail, which means the nesting is added by Review Detail's shell, not by the component. That narrows N-3 to one screen and makes it cheaper to fix.

Navigation is reachable. The 390px header keeps a visible “Menu” affordance with the lockup and notification bell. My concern was always about 768–1024, which these captures do not cover — so that remains untested rather than confirmed.

Empty-corpus mobile is exemplary — now verified rather than assumed: N/A, “No Risk Classification”, the originality denial and a 0-match chip, all at a comfortable measure, and the page simply ends. No filler panels.

Patterns worth protecting

The Topic Repository record card is the best responsive work in the product: status pill, title, keyword line, then labelled metadata in a single column, with a real pagination summary. It recomposes rather than shrinks — precisely what the contract asks for. It should be documented as the mobile list pattern and reused for Pending Reviews, My Decisions, Supervisees, User Management, Audit Log and Reports rather than each inventing its own.

PART 6

Non-design defects

Recorded separately. No visual workaround proposed for any of these — hiding them would be the wrong response.

F-1 · data contract · confirmed in pixels

Mixed similarity scales in one component

On submission #6 the fresh check reads 0.623 while its own saved snapshots — from the same session, same lecturer — read “Max similarity: 0.6%”. On seeded submission #5 the snapshots read “81.4%”.

So one formatter is receiving both 0–1 floats and 0–100 percentages. A 0.623 similarity displayed as 0.6% understates the evidence by two orders of magnitude — and 0.623 and “0.6%” appear on the same screen, ~700px apart, which is how a lecturer would discover it.

Fix the persisted scale and the formatter contract, then re-capture. Any board drawn against the current numbers would encode the bug.

And a separate design question the fix does not answer

“0.623 should render as 62.3%” is only the engineering half. A large “62.3%” is itself misleading — it invites reading as 62.3% duplicated, 62.3% original, or a 62.3% probability. It is none of those; it is a cosine distance between two embeddings.

So the design rule is independent of the scale decision: classification is primary, the number is subordinate technical provenance. Board A adopts that principle and marks the exact numeric format as blocked on the engineering contract.

F-2 · data integrity

Duplicate snapshots persisted

Submission #5 holds six snapshots, four stamped “May 22, 2026, 8:16 PM” with identical values and identical text. Submission #6 holds two nearly identical MEDIUM rows minutes apart. Either checks are being written more than once per run, or nothing de-duplicates them. This is why the history region dominates the page — a design fix alone would just hide a data problem.

F-3 · count inconsistency

“4 matches” vs “Total Matches Found 5”

Both render simultaneously on Review Detail, alongside caption text reading “The 5 most similar topics”. Most likely a historical-only count against an all-tiers total, but the UI presents them as the same quantity. Needs a definition, not a design.

F-4 · product truth in stored copy

Snapshots contain directive recommendations

Persisted snapshot text includes “Request topic modification or check with colleagues.” and “Coordinate with Dr. Adebayo before proceeding to avoid duplicate approvals.” That is the system recommending an action, stored permanently against a submission — which conflicts with “similarity evidence is advisory”. It is generated copy, so it belongs in the backend fix list, not the board list.

PART 7

Workspace principles, revised by pixels

Twelve became nine. Three were solving problems that do not exist.
W1 · rewritten and widened

Evidence is described, not rated

Was “classifications are neutral and categorical”. Pixels show that is too narrow. The full rule: the word “risk” does not appear on an evidence surface; warning iconography is reserved for genuine exceptions, never applied per-match; raw cosine is never the largest element; and no evidence surface recommends an action.

Semantic colour stays available to decisions — Approve green, Request Revision amber, Reject red — so red survives in the workspace only on controls a person operates.

W2 · new, from pixels

Absence has its own vocabulary

Generalised from the empty-corpus state, which already does this properly. Four different absences must never share a treatment: nothing eligible to compare, compared and little found, the service could not run, and nothing recorded yet.

Each states its own cause, and any absence a user might read as a pass carries the denial in words — “this does not establish originality” — not in colour.

W3 · new, from pixels

A failure never blames the user

The provider-unavailable screen leads with “Complete the form”. Rule: the topmost, largest message on any failed screen states the actual cause, an empty-state prompt never outranks an error, and the user's input survives the failure with a retry beside it.

Loss of work on failure is the one thing users never forgive.

W4 · sharpened

Two container levels, maximum

Was “cards must earn themselves” — true but not actionable enough against four-deep nesting. Hard limit: page → section → record. Nothing inside a record gets its own border. Match metadata becomes a definition list, not nested boxes. This fixes desktop ragged wrapping and the 390px measure in one move, and deletes the duplicate summary card by construction.

W5 · new, from pixels

The human decision outranks the machine

On any resolved record the recorded decision — outcome, rationale, decider, date — is the first and most prominent block. Evidence history collapses behind a summary line (“6 checks · latest 23 May”). Disabled controls do not occupy the primary position on a record that can no longer be acted on.

Stored rationale never shares styling with a system note.

W6 · retained

No fixed inner columns

The three named widths hold up in pixels. What breaks is the fixed column inside them. Rule: no fixed-px inner column, and no side column that can outlive its content. An action belongs adjacent to the thing it produces — “Run Similarity Check” sits with the evidence, not stranded in a gutter.

W7 · sharpened

One bold run per record

Pixels show the real damage is not page titles but label bolding: four bold labels inside one match card leave nothing subordinate. Labels go small, uppercase-optional and unbolded; the record title is the only 600-weight element. Nothing above 600 anywhere.

W8 · retained, narrowed

One palette; no glyph fonts as icons

Tokens only, gold for focus. Added from pixels: no emoji as UI iconography, anywhere. And accent colour is never assigned to a neutral lifecycle bucket — “Under review” is not a red state.

W9 · retained

Say each fact once

Pixels justify this far more strongly than source did: the same three values appear three times on Review Detail, two chips restate one count on Pending Reviews, two panels restate one absence on empty-corpus. One statement per fact per screen — and where two counts differ, resolve the definition rather than showing both.

Withdrawn — pixels showed no problem to solve

“Tables recompose, never scroll” — already true; becomes a documented convention, not a principle. “Control and text floors” (desktop half) — desktop controls measure ~40px and read fine; only the mobile question survives, as a measurement task. “Breakpoints match the contract” — 390 and 1440 both behave well, and the 1151px concern lives entirely at 768–1024, which these captures do not cover. I am not going to legislate a breakpoint I have not seen fail.

Still deliberately absent: no marketing display type, no 96/128 rhythm, no dark-to-paper crossing, no hero framing. The workspace captures are calm and functional and should stay that way.

Board A · first, blocks the others

Similarity evidence — full state matrix
