Board B · lecturer review detail · recomposition

One axis, two hierarchies

Same content, same product behaviour, same Board A evidence system. What changes is geometry and — on a decided record — which party the page is about. All values are the real captured ones from submissions #6 and #5.

The five pixel-established problems
P-1 ~1,100px dead left gutter beside a full-height evidence column.
N-3 four container levels wrapping a component that sits happily in two on Check My Topic.
History six near-identical rows consuming ~1,000px.
Inversion on a decided record the stored rationale is last, below disabled buttons, styled as a system note.
Hierarchy machine evidence outweighs the human decision.
What this board changes

A single vertical axis at record width (1184px). No side column, so no gutter can outlive its content — “Run a new check” moves inside the evidence block where its output appears.

Two container levels. Page → section. Nothing inside a section gets its own border, which is what restores the measure at both 1440 and 390.

History becomes a register — one row per check at ~40px instead of ~165px. Nothing removed, nothing grouped away.

B2 reorders the page so the recorded decision is the first and largest thing on it.

Preserved exactly

Proposal title and every structured-context field including the “Not provided” honesty · the frozen Board A evidence system · full evidence provenance and history · the rationale requirement and its copy · Approve / Request Revision / Reject · the recorded decision with decider and timestamp · the disabled-state explanation · all boundary copy.

No product behaviour changes. Nothing here needs a new endpoint, a new field, or new aggregation — with one exception I flag explicitly rather than assume.

B1 notes

The gutter is gone because the column is gone. Provenance sits beside the classification only where it has content to sit beside — inside the evidence block, which is always tall enough to support it. “Run a new check” now sits with the records it produces, and states what it does and does not do.

Context is a ruled grid, not seven bordered boxes. Same seven fields, same honesty, roughly 40% of the height. On the email I had the causality backwards. overflow-wrap: anywhere permits a break at any character — it is the mechanism that produces “edu.n / g” on the captured queue, not the one that prevents it. The real fix is room: overflow-wrap: break-word as a last resort only, and at 390px the email takes its own stacked row — label above, value below, the full 358px — so it never needs to break at all.

The decision block is the only coloured region and the only one with a 2px rule above it. The rationale field drops from ~1,140px to a 720px measure. Buttons go to 44px. Decision colour semantics are unchanged from today — and per N-4, confirming Request Revision uses amber, not Approve-green.

One deliberate departure from the current app. The rationale placeholder moves off the app's #9ca3af (2.54:1) to #6b7280 (4.83:1). Board A's frozen R8 admits no contrast exception, and I would rather change the value than carve one out — the low-contrast placeholder is part of what audit finding A-1 flagged, so reproducing it inside a proposal would be inheriting a defect rather than recreating a style. The greyed blocks elsewhere on these boards keep the original grey, because those are recreations.

Evidence history collapses to one line by default — count, classification, latest timestamp — with its own advisory sentence retained. Total page height falls from roughly 2,400px to about 1,150px, with nothing removed.

Four of these six rows are identical, to the minute. That is F-2 — duplicate persisted snapshots — and the register makes it more visible, not less. That is deliberate: the honest compression is row density, not merging. See the strategy comparison below.

B2 notes — what the reordering asserts

Identity first, then the decision. A compact topic-title header sits above the decision record, so the decision is never detached from the thing decided — but it stays a header, not a section: eyebrow, title, status pill, back link, and no context grid. The decision record keeps the 3px edge rule and remains the dominant substantive content.

The page is otherwise about the decision, not the check. Outcome at 27px, decider and timestamp on the same line, and the rationale at 17px directly beneath — the first substantive thing read on a terminal record is what a person decided and why. In the captured build that same sentence is the last element on the page, in a blue info callout below three disabled buttons.

The disabled buttons are gone entirely. They occupied the primary position on a record where no decision can be taken; the explanation copy is preserved verbatim as a quiet line inside the decision block. Nothing is lost — a control that cannot be used is not information. If reopening or amending a decision is ever supported, that action belongs here as a single live control, not as three greyed ones.

Red appears twice and both are legitimate: the status pill and the 3px edge rule, both marking a decision a human recorded. The evidence block below stays fully neutral, so the Board A separation survives inversion — the reordering does not smuggle verdict colour back in.

Evidence is reframed, not demoted. It reads “Latest saved similarity check” with its own timestamp — stating what the record is, and stopping short of tying it to the decision. An earlier draft used past tense here (“before the decision”, “were on file at the time of”) to convey that the evidence preceded the judgement; that phrasing asserted a snapshot-to-decision relationship the data contract does not establish, so it is gone. Sequence now comes from the two timestamps sitting on their own blocks, which is all the UI can honestly claim. The full register stays on the page. Page height falls from roughly 2,600px to about 1,500px.

HISTORY COMPRESSION

Four strategies, and why I chose the least clever one

Chosen · compact register

One row per check at ~40px. Six rows plus a header = ~320px, against ~1,000px today. Summary line above; register expanded on a terminal record, collapsed while pending.

Needs no backend change and hides nothing. Every row, timestamp, tier count and author stays visible and auditable. The compression is purely typographic.

Cost: the duplicate rows are still repetitive to read. I consider that correct — they are a data defect and should look like one.

Available · grouped runs

Collapse consecutive checks with identical classification, cosine, tier counts and minute into “4 identical checks · 22 May 20:16”, expandable to all four.

No new backend needed — it is client-side grouping of records already returned, so no invented aggregation. Would cut the register to three rows.

Rejected for now: it makes F-2 invisible at exactly the moment someone might otherwise notice and fix it. Worth adopting after F-2 is resolved, when a genuine run of repeated checks would be a real user action rather than a bug.

Considered · latest + archive

Show only the most recent check inline; move the rest behind “View all 6 checks”, possibly on its own route.

Rejected: the value of history here is seeing whether evidence changed between checks. Hiding all but the latest removes the only reason to keep a history at all, and pushes audit information a click away on a record that exists for accountability.

Considered · change-only register

List only checks whose result differed from the one before, with “no change” runs summarised.

Rejected: the most compact option and the least honest. An audit register that omits events is no longer an audit register — and deciding what counts as “a change” is a data-semantics judgement the UI has no business making.

Data dependencies — flagged, not assumed

Nothing in B1 or B2 requires new backend aggregation. Both compose fields the captures prove are already returned: classification, cosine, tier counts, checked-at, checked-by, and the decision's outcome, author, timestamp and rationale.

Two unverifiable claims removed rather than hedged. B2's evidence block now reads simply “Latest saved similarity check” with its own timestamp. The earlier “before the decision” phrasing asserted a relationship between a snapshot and a decision that no data contract I can see establishes — ordering by timestamp is not linkage, and a reader would have taken it as provenance. The decision's own timestamp stays attached to the decision, independently. Likewise the “all Higher similarity” / “both Moderate similarity” summaries are gone: they were client-side aggregations over rows that may not all be loaded or classified when the summary renders, so the summary lines now carry only counts and dates — facts the UI holds unconditionally.

One question still open: whether a decision can ever be amended or reopened. B2 leaves room for that control but does not draw one, because no capture or route shows it exists.

F-1 remains visible rather than smoothed over. The stored value on this submission is “81.4%” while a fresh check on #6 gives 0.623 — the register shows a normalised figure with a flag and a plain statement that the format is an engineering decision. I have not picked a format to make the board look finished.

What Board B does not settle

Whether removing the disabled buttons is right. I argue a control that cannot be used is not information, and its explanation is preserved. But if lecturers currently use those greyed buttons as a status cue — “this is the screen where decisions happen, and it is closed” — then removing them loses something the copy alone may not replace. That is a question for a lecturer, not for me.

Whether B2 should show the proposal above the evidence at all. I placed it second on the grounds that a decision is meaningless without the thing decided. An alternative — decision, then evidence, then proposal — would be more compact but reads as though the machine's finding matters more than the topic. I would want to watch someone use it before committing.

The 768–1024 range. No capture covers it and I have not designed for it. The single-axis composition should degrade cleanly — there is no side column to collapse — but that is a prediction, not a verified claim.
