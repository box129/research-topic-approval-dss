Step 1 · audit corrections applied to v2
1 · Empty corpus → protect with small polish

Two real faults now recorded, not cosmetic ones. “No meaningful matches were returned by this check” states the wrong fact — it describes a comparison that ran; none did. And that panel is mint-tinted, so the state most needing to avoid an all-clear reading carries the approval hue. The N/A · No Risk Classification · NOT CLASSIFIED · neutral framing · originality denial all stay protected.

2 · “Protect all advisory copy” split in two

Boundary copy defends human authority and is protected. Directive machine advice — “Request topic modification or check with colleagues”, “Coordinate with Dr. Adebayo before proceeding” — usurps it and must be rewritten or removed. My v1 wording conflated them.

3 · Board C scoped to evidenced states only

The “three loading skeletons” are withdrawn — I proposed them from design-system instinct, not evidence. No capture shows a loading state. Board C is now provider unavailable · no eligible corpus · the captured validation error · any confirmed “nothing recorded” state.

4 · The number is two problems, not one

F-1 stays an engineering contract defect. Separately: even a correct “62.3%” invites reading as 62.3% duplicated, original, or probable — it is none of those. So classification is primary and the number is subordinate technical provenance, independent of how the scale resolves.

Board A rationale · the problem being solved

The pixels showed a surface that asserts a verdict five different ways at once: the word Risk, a red/green traffic light, a ⚠ on every result including “Low Match”, raw cosine as the largest figure on screen, and stored copy telling the lecturer what to do. Any one of those alone would undermine “similarity evidence is advisory”. Together they overwhelm it.

The hard part is not removing the verdict — it is keeping the urgency. A neutral HIGH that reads as “nothing to see here” would be a worse failure than a red one, because a lecturer would stop looking. So this board moves urgency out of hue and into three things colour was doing badly anyway: fill weight, how much is shown by default, and a sentence that names the consequence. Higher similarity gets the heaviest chip, its records expanded, and a lead line that says read them. Lower similarity gets the lightest chip, records collapsed, and the originality denial.

Preserved: Check My Topic's mobile composition (already good), structured topic context per record, the empty-corpus honesty as the semantic anchor, the advisory callout, provenance fields the API actually returns, existing control sizing, and the console/record/workflow widths.

Embedding notes — this is the test the board had to pass

Look at the greyed blocks against the live one. Across A1–A4 the evidence component carries no resting colour — not in the classification, not in the records, and not in its affordances, which rest in underlined ink rather than the app's action-green. Keyboard focus is the exception and stays on the global focus system. So on this page the only colour belongs to the decision, and a lecturer can tell machine from person at a glance — which is exactly what the current build cannot do, because its evidence banner is the most colourful thing on screen.

A5 is the one exception, and a principled one. A service failure is a genuine warning, so it earns an amber edge rule — the only non-neutral mark anywhere in the component, and it appears when the system has failed rather than when it has found something. That is the distinction the current build loses by tinting findings amber and red.

Four things collapse in the lecturer context: the duplicate summary card is gone (N-2), the banner and the summary become one block, the record list defaults to one row with the rest behind a disclosure, and “Run a new check” moves into the evidence block where its output appears — retiring the ~1,100px dead gutter that stranded it mid-page (P-1). The component drops from roughly 1,200px of page height to about 380px, which is what makes room for Board B to put the decision first.

COMPONENT RULES

Nine rules the system runs on

R1 · Classification

One neutral hue, three fill weights — filled ink / tinted / outline — plus a dashed outline for not classified. Plain-language label first at 14px, the API token second as a mono chip at 12.5px. Never red, amber or green. Distinguishable in greyscale and by label alone.

Extends to resting affordances. Similarity evidence must not use approval/action green as a resting semantic treatment — so links and buttons inside the component rest in ink, because the app's action colour is the same emerald as Approve. Interactive focus and keyboard states may follow the global accessible focus system. Visible keyboard focus is never weakened or removed to keep the component neutral. Scoped to this component only; the workspace keeps its emerald everywhere else.

R2 · Numbers

Cosine lives in the provenance strip and the per-record meta line, in mono at 13px — never a display figure. Always labelled “cosine” and always accompanied by the disclaimer that it is not a percentage of duplication, originality or probability. Exact format is blocked on the F-1 engineering contract; the board shows the raw stored float and does not design around the current inconsistency.

Subordinate does not mean unreadable — the distinction R2 turns on. The disclaimer is the one sentence preventing the cosine being read as a probability, so it sits at 13.5px and 4.5:1 minimum, not at the 2.5:1 grey it would be easy to give it. A subordinate mark is smaller and quieter than the classification; it is never fainter than legible.

R3 · Icons

No icon on any similarity result. Rank ordinals replace the ⚠, at 13–13.5px in medium weight — a signal that replaces an icon must not be the faintest mark on its row. Warning and error iconography is reserved for genuine system failures — in this board, only A5. No emoji anywhere (N-1).

R4 · Result records

A bordered list, not a card grid — so no ragged dead cells and every title gets full measure. Two container levels maximum; nothing inside a record gets its own border. One 600-weight run per record (the title); labels are colour-differentiated, never bold. Provenance shown only where the API supplies it: title, population, location, study focus, supervisor, session, source tier, cosine. No field-level semantic attribution — the model does not score fields individually and the UI must not imply it does.

R5 · Disclosure as emphasis

Higher similarity opens all records; moderate and lower open the top record only. This is the mechanism that replaces colour-as-urgency: the interface allocates attention instead of issuing a rating. The count is always visible so nothing is hidden — only deferred.

R6 · Absence

Every absence states its own cause. No eligible corpus means nothing was compared and says so; it is never described as a comparison that found little. Absence is never green or mint, and any absence a reader could mistake for a pass carries the originality denial in words. Empty states add no filler — the page ends.

R7 · Failure

The topmost and largest message states the actual cause. An empty-state prompt must never outrank an error, and a failure never implies user error. The user's input survives with a retry beside it. Warning colour appears as an edge rule, not a filled tint, so a service fault cannot be mistaken for a finding.

R8 · Type floor

Every text class in the component, stated so it can be checked: classification label 14px · record title 15–15.5px · context values 14px · meta and provenance 13.5px · disclaimer 13.5px · rank 13–13.5px · mono token 12.5px · section eyebrows 11–11.5px in medium weight. Nothing below 4.5:1 against its own background. No task-essential or interpretive information is below 13px. 11–12.5px is reserved for nonessential structural labels and technical metadata that is not required to understand or act on the result. The v2 audit filed 12px-carrying-real-information as A-1; this board is bound by its own finding.

R9 · Copy — the governing rule

The system reports what it found and never prescribes an academic action. No “request modification”, no “coordinate with”, no “proceed”. “Risk” does not appear on an evidence surface. “Match” is not used for a nearest neighbour. Every evidence surface carries the human-authority boundary once — and once only.

The honest risk in this board

A greyscale HIGH could read as inert. I have tried to prevent that with fill weight, full disclosure and an explicit lead sentence — but that is the one judgement in this board that pixels alone cannot settle. It needs a lecturer and a student to look at A1 and A3 and say whether A1 makes them want to read the records. If it does not, the fix is more typographic contrast in the lead line, not the return of red.
